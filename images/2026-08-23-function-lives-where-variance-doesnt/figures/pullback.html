Left: a two-dimensional state cloud read by a three-token softmax head. The cloud is stretched along the vertical axis, which holds 96 percent of the variance, but the head barely reads it. The grey circle is every step of the same Euclidean length: this is all that plain reconstruction error can see. The blue ellipse is the Fisher pullback's answer: every step on it moves the output distribution by the same amount. The same-length step moves the answer about 60 times more along the loud axis than along the quiet one. Right: the head's output before (solid) and after (outline) the probe step. Drag the state to the right: the output saturates, the metric shrinks, and the equal-movement ellipse balloons off the plot. The prediction ½δᵀGδ is a small-step approximation. It is tight near the balanced state and overshoots when a step this size hits saturation.
